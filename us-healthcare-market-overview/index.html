💡 Interesting Facts about US Healthcare

  • ⚠️ Highest Spending, Poorer Outcomes: The US spends the most but lags behind in basic health outcomes.
  • 🧬 Chronic Conditions Drives Costs: The majority of spending is on long-term chronic disease management.
  • 💊 Skyrocketing Drug Spending: $600 billion is spent on prescription drugs alone.
  • 🗑️ Huge Waste and Administrative Inefficiencies: Complexity drives up non-care costs.
  • 🏥 Hospitals and Infrastructure: The system boasts 6,093 hospitals, 900,000 staffed beds, and 34 million hospital admissions annually.
  • 🚧 Access Barriers Persist: 7.3% of U.S. adults skipped needed medical care in 2024 due to cost.

📊 Characteristics Of US Healthcare

  • One of the largest healthcare systems in the world & arguably the most complex - largely owned and operated by private business.
  • Has No Universal Health Program, unlike most developed nations.
  • Healthcare Spend has outpaced the growth rate in the gross domestic product (GDP).
  • Predominance of Private insurance: Coverage provided primarily by employers.
  • Mixed Healthcare System: Consists of public and private, for-profit and non-profit insurers & health care providers.
  • Benefit packages and cost-sharing structure: Offered by public & private insurers governed by federal and state regulations.
  • The 2019 WHO survey of the healthcare systems found the US healthcare system to be the most expensive and worst-performing.
  • Extremely Complex Adaptive System with each independent agent acting in their own perceived interest with no charge.

🏛️ Role of Federal Government

  • The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) is responsible for public health in the US.
  • Government provides National insurance coverage program via Medicare (For 65 yrs. and above. Also for less than 65 yrs. with disabilities/ESRD).
  • Medicare dominates & sets benchmark for others on healthcare payments.
  • Medicaid is a state-based insurance coverage program, jointly adopted by state and federal (who sets standards), for Children & the low-income citizens.
  • Federal govt also: Surveys Health Status & Needs, Sets Policies & Standards, Passes Laws & Regulations, and Supports Research.
  • 18% of US Population are covered under the Medicare & 21% under the Medicaid + CHIP.

📊 US Healthcare Spendings

🔄 Flow of Funds in US Healthcare

🔄 Flow of Funds Details

Funds flow from US Individuals (via State/Federal Taxes, Paycheck Deductions, Private Purchase, or Out-of-pocket spend) to Private/Public Healthcare Insurance and eventually to Healthcare Providers.

📈 Most Expensive Stats

  • US per capita healthcare spend quadrupled (from 3.6% in 1980 to 16.9% in 2018).
  • Very high administrative costs — 8% of its healthcare dollar is spent on admin costs, compared to 1% to 3% in other countries.
  • 30% of total spend goes to unnecessary, ineffective, overpriced & wasteful services.
  • Americans spend 4 times on prescriptive drugs compared to average of other countries.
  • Hospitals work as profit centers and account for 33% of the nation's healthcare costs.
  • Older Americans accounting for 16% of the population increase the healthcare cost.

📊 Stats (as of 2024):

  • 8.2% have no health insurance.
  • >1M went overseas to get a better deal.
  • 9% declared bankrupt due to high medical bills.

🛑 US Healthcare Expensive: Causes

"The single greatest cause of rising healthcare spending in the U.S. is the growing prevalence of chronic disease."

"The current healthcare delivery system is designed for treating acute illnesses."

🔵 Acute Medical Problems

  • Curable, self-limited.
  • E.g. Strep throat, Appendicitis.

🔴 Chronic Medical Problems

  • Incurable, requires control.
  • E.g. Diabetes, Hypertension.

⚠️ Chronic Disease is Different

  • Last for a lifetime.
  • Cause each other.
  • Behavior often causal.
  • Behavior change is crucial to success.
  • Requires a different care system.
  • Wellness and prevention are key.

🌟 Pros of US Healthcare

  • United States has been the global leader in health technology Innovation, with market size of almost $120 billion reigning in biotechnology.
  • The US scores high on innovation, patient-centered care, and preventive health measures.
  • Physicians and Nurses are much better paid in the US, thus preventing strikes and demonstrations for high pay like in Germany and elsewhere.
  • The US has some of the best doctors and hospitals in the world, for those who can afford them.
  • Americans are never checked by government for using too much care like that of government owned healthcare like Taiwan or UK.
  • Patient wait times are much better in the USA in comparison with patients in the UK, France, Sweden and Canada due to bureaucratic government processes.
  • Mixed Payer markets & threat of lawsuit drives them to stay cutting edge with latest technology and best trained professionals.

⚖️ Regulatory and Influencing Bodies

🏢 Department of Health and Human Services (HHS)

The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) is the U.S. Government's principal agency for protecting the health of all Americans and providing essential human services. It has 11 operating agencies. Primary ones as below:

📋 Regulatory Bodies (1/2) — Primary HHS Agencies

🏛️ CMS (Center for Medicare & Medicaid)

  • Administers nation's healthcare programs.
  • Medicare, Medicaid & CHIP and oversees most of the regulations related directly to the healthcare system.

💊 FDA (Food and Drug Administration)

  • Responsible for protecting & promoting public health via the regulation of food safety, tobacco products, dietary supplements, medicine, medical devices, biological products etc.

🦠 CDC (Center for Disease Control & Prevention)

  • Protects the public health of the nation by providing leadership and direction in the prevention and control of diseases and responding to public health emergencies.

💻 ONC for HIT (Office of the National Coordinator for Health IT)

  • Devoted to the implementation of HIT for the nationwide shift from paper to electronic medical record.
  • Promotes, assists and governs the health information exchange (HIE) process.

📋 Regulatory Bodies (2/2)

  • SAMHSA (Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration) — Charged with improving the quality and availability of treatment and rehabilitative services in order to reduce illness, death, disability, and the cost to society resulting from substance abuse and mental illnesses.
  • ONC (The Office of the National Coordinator for Health IT) — The ONC's mission involves many aspects of health information technology (HIT), including policy coordination, strategic planning for the adoption of health IT and health information exchanges (HIE), establishing governance for the Nationwide Health Information Network and promoting a national health IT infrastructure.
  • HITSP (Health Information Technology Standards Panel) — An organization created to promote standardization and broad scale interoperability among healthcare applications and information systems.

✅ Quality & Accreditation Agencies

🔬 AHRQ

Agency of Healthcare Research and Quality — Conducts research aimed at improving the quality of healthcare, reducing its costs, and addressing patient safety and medical errors.

🏥 JCAHO

The Joint Commission on Accreditation of Healthcare Organizations — A non-for-profit organization which employs a system of examining and accrediting healthcare organizations, which has an impact on their reimbursement from CMS.

📋 NCQA

National Committee for Quality Assurance — A non-for-profit organization which ensures the quality of the managed care plans. It also provides accreditation for newer organizational models e.g. ACOs and PCMHs.

📊 NQF

National Quality Forum — A non-for-profit organization which sets goals for performance management, endorses standards used for measuring performance and promotes educational and outreach programs.

⚙️ Standards Setting & Influencing Bodies

🔗 HL7 (Health Level Seven)

A not-for-profit, ANSI-accredited standards organization. The focus of HL7 work is in supplying a structure for the development and electronic distribution of health information that is used in clinical practice.

🌐 IHE (Integrating the Healthcare Enterprise)

An international organization that focuses on the development of open and global Integration Profiles and on the regional deployment of interoperable IT systems.

📖 IHTSDO

International Health Terminology Standards Developing Organization — A non-for-profit organization which owns SNOMED-CT, the most comprehensive, multilingual clinical terminology in the world.

🖼️ DICOM

Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine — A standard for handling, storing, printing, and transmitting information in medical imaging.
